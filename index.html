01

Don't start where everyone tells you to start

Creative 1, v2Walking talking head60sBo
Draft

Sixty seconds, walking, founder to camera. The argument: everyone automates the task that chews the most time, it breaks by week three, and the real problem is a layer underneath in the data that task reaches into. Lands on the audit as the thing that maps it first.

The visual loop is the rolled A1 process map, under his arm from the first frame and unrolled at 30s. That's what stops it being a man walking and talking for a minute.

0 — 5s Hook Already walking, map already under his arm.

"If you're an Australian business owner who knows you need AI and has genuinely no idea where to start, don't start where everyone's telling you to start."

half beat, keeps walking

"That's the reason it doesn't work."

On screen: Bo [surname], founder — first 3 seconds only

5 — 16s Act 1 States the common-sense approach fairly.

"Everyone starts with the task. You pick the thing chewing the most time. The quoting. The data entry. Chasing invoices. And you automate that one."

beat

"Sounds like common sense, doesn't it."

16 — 30s Act 2 The breakage, then the reframe.

"So here's what actually happens. It runs fine for about three weeks. Then it breaks. Then it breaks again, and again… And by the fifth time it breaks nobody believes it anymore and nobody's confident using it."

beat

"Because the task was never the problem. The task is just where you could see it. The problem's a layer underneath, in the data that task is reaching into."

30 — 45s Act 3 Unroll the top of the map here.

"We've been through seventeen Australian businesses looking for exactly this. Builders, real estate offices, NDIS providers, marketing agencies, field teams running vans."

"Every single one was leaking money. Smallest was sixty-seven thousand a year. Biggest was nine hundred and fifty-eight thousand."

beat, holds up the map

"And you can't automate a single bit of it until somebody's written down how the business actually runs."

VisualThe map comes off his arm and the top of it unrolls on this beat. It's been in frame unexplained for thirty seconds, so this is the moment it pays off.
45 — 55s The AI audit Names the offer in one breath.

"And that's what the AI audit is. We map the whole business first. Every process, every leak counted in dollars, ranked in the order you should actually build it."

55 — 60s CTA Guarantee, then the click.

"And if the audit doesn't find at least three times what it costs in wasted money, you don't pay and you keep the map."

"So click learn more below and I'll show you which layer your business is stuck on."

The argument: everybody treats this as a technology problem and goes shopping for technology, when most of the work is writing down how the business actually runs. The line the whole thing rests on is "you cannot automate a process nobody has ever written down." Whatever else changes, that survives.

Beat 1 The two rooms Everyone watching is in one of them.

"You've either put AI into your business and watched it fall over, or you haven't started because nobody can tell you where to begin so you get overwhelmed with the amount of options and you quit."

beat

Beat 2 The specifics Three images, no abstraction.

"Right now someone on your team is re-entering the same job into two different systems. A quote that should have gone out Monday is sitting with you, because everything sits with you. And somewhere there's a ChatGPT subscription nobody touches and a CRM with an AI button that has never saved anyone an hour."

beat

DeliveryThree concrete pictures in a row, each one something the viewer can check against their own week. Don't rush between them.

"Maybe you've already paid for the bigger version. A workshop. A freelancer's automation that broke every fortnight until he stopped answering."

"The reason this keeps happening is that everybody treats this as a technology problem. So everybody goes shopping for technology."

beat

Beat 4 The real work The thesis. Protect this.

"Most of the work is writing down how your business actually runs. Every step, every handover, and all the decisions your people make without thinking about them."

"You cannot automate a process nobody has ever written down."

beat

DeliveryLand the second line on its own and stop. Let it sit before moving into beat 5.

"And this is why, if you don't know where to start, that problem gets solved immediately. Because after this you can stop guessing and start implementing."
